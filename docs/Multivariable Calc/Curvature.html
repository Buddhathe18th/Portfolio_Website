Curvature

Given a vector-valued function that outputs a smooth curve, we can image a car driving along this curve. Imagine at a certain point, the steering wheel locks, and the driver starts to drive in a circle. If the point on the circle was a tight turn, then the circle the driver would turn in would have a very small radius, but if the curve is very straight, the circle would have a very large radius. If the curve was just a line, then the car wouldn't drive in a circle either way,

The concept of curvature is a way to quantify how much a curve will curve. More rigurously, we're trying to find the radius of the circle that hugs the circle the closest. We'll denote the curvature as \(\kappa\), pronounced kappa. \(\kappa\) will be the 1 over the radius of the curvature. This is intuitive since when the radius of the circle is very large, we're curving less, and when the radius is close to 0, we're turning a lot more. Numerically, the curvature is defined as \(\kappa = \lVert \dfrac{dT}{ds} \lVert\) where \(T\) is the unit tangent vector and \(s\) is the arc length. This is because we want to see how much change in the direction of the tangent for a small change in the arc length. Since we are looking at the change of the unit tangent vector, the only change is the direction change. Now let's go through the steps of computing the curvature.

Let's try to find the curvature for a function \(f\left(x\right)=\begin{bmatrix}x-\sin\left(x\right)\\x^2-\cos\left(x\right)\end{bmatrix}\)

Finding the unit tangent vector

At any given \(x\), the derivative of \(f\left(x\right)\) is equal to \(\begin{bmatrix}1-\cos\left(x\right)\\2x+\sin\left(x\right)\end{bmatrix}\)
