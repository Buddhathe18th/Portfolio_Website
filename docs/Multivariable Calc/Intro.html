Introduction to Multivariable Calculus

In this little series of blogs, I wish to cover the basics and some interesting concepts learnt in multivariable calculus. This is something that I am just covering myself, through other online sources, just like the one I'm creating now. One big difficulty I had with this topic was the intuition behind certain formulas, which really held me back from understanding. I hope that these blogs can help you with at least the intuition behind the numbers and formulas, or just see another perspective.

Prerequisites

Since these blogs are not designed to be super high-leveled, there isn't too much to know before hand. Some things to know are how to take derivatives of simple one-variable expressions, integrate them, and most importantly, the intuition behind it. In addition, a little knowledge on vectors and matrices will help you prepare for some of this content, especially operators such as dot products, cross products, matrix multiplication and such.

What is multivariable calculus?

Before now, you might be used to functions with one input and one output, such as \(f\left(x\right)=x^2,g\left(x\right)=\ln\left(\cos\left(x\right)\right)\). In multivariable calculus, instead of a single output, we will deal with functions that have multiple inputs, and outputs in both a single value, and also vectors. Don't worry is this is all too much to understand at once, we will get to it all in due time.

How can functions have multiple variables?

Imagine this, you have a money counting machine, where you put in some amount of coins, and it counts the total amount of money inputted. In this case, the machine only takes in coins, so you can only input (assumining you live in Canada, feel free to change these to your on currency :D) nickels, dimes, quarters, loonies and toonies. Let the number of nickels, dimes, quarters, loonies and toonies inputted be \(n,d,q,l,t\) respectively. Then the machine, or this function, with five inputs will output \(0.05n+0.1d+0.25q+l+2t\). This is simply what a multivariable function is.

Another big issue with understading multivariable functions is how to visualize them. With single variable function, for example \(f\left(x\right)=x^2\), we can easily imagine them as a two dimensional graph, with the x-axis as the input, and y-axis as the output. With multiple variables, it is harder to visualize the graph, since with \(n\) inputs, we will need a \(n+1\) dimentional graph. We can only simply visualize up to a 3d graph, so the graphical intuition of multivariable calculus will become harder past two variable functions. As a side note, most people know about Desmos, which is a great tool for graphing two dimensional graphs, but most people dont know about Desmos 3d, which can be a useful tool in visualizing 3d graphs.
